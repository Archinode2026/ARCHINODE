Why Korea

A Market You Can't Ignore

South Korea is the world's fastest-growing premium design market. With a consumer base that adopts European design trends in 1–2 years (vs. 3–5 years elsewhere in Asia), Korea offers unmatched opportunities for architecture and design brands.

Rapid Trend Adoption

Korean consumers are early adopters of global design trends. Trends that take 3–5 years to spread in Europe reach mainstream Korea in just 1–2 years.

Renovation Boom

Millions of apartments built in the 1980s–90s are entering renovation cycles, driving massive demand for premium finishes, fixtures, and furniture.

Design-Savvy Consumers

Korean millennials and Gen Z invest heavily in their living spaces. Interior design is a cultural priority, not just a luxury.

Gateway to Asia

Success in Korea validates your brand across Asia. Korean taste is a benchmark for China, Japan, and Southeast Asian markets.

Professional Network

Korea has over 50,000 registered architects and 30,000+ interior design firms actively specifying international products.

Premium Willingness

Korean consumers are willing to pay premium prices for European quality. The “Made in Italy” and “Made in Germany” labels carry strong brand equity.

Trade Fair Entry

You cannot import your own samples into Korea

Korean customs require a resident business to be the importer of record. A brand without a Korean company has no one to clear its own booth — not the samples, not the display units, not the fixtures. That is where most European brands stop. We take you from there to a stand on the floor.

  1. Step zero

    Can you enter at all?

    Before anything else: which of your products are a certified category in Korea, which are exempt as exhibition goods, and what the document chain looks like. We answer this before your production schedule depends on it. No charge.

  2. The importer of record

    We import under our own business registration. This is the step a brand cannot do for itself, and it is the reason the rest is possible.

  3. In, and out again

    Carnet, temporary import, or a formal import — the right route depends on what happens to the goods after the show: back to Europe, held for a buyer, or sold on the floor. We decide that with you first, so the exhibition does not end with a customs problem.

  4. The stand

    We arrange the space and build it. Not a shell scheme with your logo on it — a stand designed for a Korean buyer's walking line and a Korean photographer's camera.

  5. The show floor

    Someone who speaks both languages stands at your stand. Enquiries are written down, not remembered.

  6. After the show

    The enquiries you collected are sorted and handed over, and your brand page goes up here — so the people who met you at the stand can find you again.

Lead time and cost depend on the fair and on what you make — lighting and electrical goods need more notice than furniture. Tell us which fair you are looking at and we will come back with a schedule and a figure.
